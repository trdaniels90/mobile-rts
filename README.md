# Red Horizon

A Cold War real-time strategy game of Allies versus Soviets, in the pre-rendered 3D pixel-art style of the mid-90s classics. It runs in any modern browser: iPhone and iPad (Safari), Android and desktop. It is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript on a canvas, with no build step and no dependencies. All art is generated in code: every unit, structure, tree and ore pile is a small voxel model rendered with one sun, shading, metal and glass highlights and a cast shadow, then drawn as pixel art. No original game assets are used.

## How it plays

- Start with a Construction Yard and $5000. Build from the sidebar: **Power Plant → Ore Refinery → Barracks → War Factory → Helipad / Airfield**, plus defences. New structures can go up to 6 cells from any of your buildings. The War Factory also builds the **Mobile Outpost**: drive it out to a distant ore field, tap DEPLOY, and it unpacks into an Outpost you can build around (8 cells), so a refinery can sit right by the ore instead of trucks crossing the map; the computer does the same. Expansions are fair game: one auto-attack wave in three (and some aircraft) raids an enemy Outpost or outlying refinery instead of the main base, and any wave that passes close to one diverts to destroy it. Structure repairs take about a minute from nearly wrecked to whole (a full repair costs half the structure's price) and run at a quarter speed while under fire; the computer only repairs structures nothing is shooting at. The minimap radar only works with a **Radar Dome** ($1000, needs an Ore Refinery): with none built it shows static, and low power takes it offline. The Radar Dome also unlocks each side's long-range support vehicle at the War Factory: the Allied **Artillery** (a tracked howitzer, $600) and the Soviet **V2 Rocket Launcher** ($800). Both outrange every defence, have no turret (the whole vehicle turns to aim) and lob their rounds in a high arc, so they wreck anything standing still but can miss a moving target, and they are lightly armoured. Once you have a War Factory, the **Advanced Power Plant** ($500, 3x3) gives twice the power of a standard one.
- **Ore is automatic.** Every refinery comes with an Ore Truck that finds ore, harvests it and brings it home by itself; a truck that comes under fire runs home, unloads and waits by its refinery until the danger has passed, then goes back to work. Each trip it picks a field with plenty of ore left that is close by and not already crowded with trucks (of either side), mines its richest cells, and leaves scraped-out fields to regrow; nearby gem fields come first. Ore fields slowly regrow.
- **The map** takes its layout from the classic Red Alert map *A Path Beyond*: an island where two long lakes cut across it from corner to corner. Armies cross over the land gap in the centre (the main road), over a timber bridge across either lake, or round the far ends of the lakes. It is sized for 2v2 (112 x 112 cells) with eight start positions, four on each side of the lakes: a corner, one by each bridge and one forward on the main road. Each game you start at a random one, and the enemy at a random one on the far side of the lakes, never on your side. Every start position has a wide stretch of open ground (14 cells all round) for building, with two ore fields of its own just beyond it, so the empty ones make good expansions for a Mobile Outpost, and more ore lies on the lake shores between the crossings. Each side also has one small field of rare gems out in the contested middle: a truck load of gems is worth twice as much as ore, but gems never grow back. Stands of forest grow in clumps along the coast, the lake shores and out in the fields, kept clear of the roads, bases and ore, and every ore field (gems included) always has a way in. Woods, rocks and shorelines vary each game; the layout is mirrored so both sides are equal.
- **Your army.** New units spread out to random open spots around your base and guard it (where they stand is up to chance, so position them yourself). *Auto Attack* starts off; switch it on and, once 8 are waiting, they all head out at once from where they stand, each at its own top speed, for the enemy Construction Yard (every third wave raids an outlying Outpost or refinery), fighting whatever they meet on the way (defences before other buildings). The computer's waves march in formation instead: a wave halts to fight anything it meets, every member joins in, and a hit on any of them turns the whole wave on the shooter, even artillery firing from beyond sight; then it marches on. Units guarding or on the attack likewise turn on whoever shoots them, unless already trading fire with an enemy in range. Move and Attack orders are followed to the letter. The AUTO button shows how many are waiting; the game remembers whether you left it on. A new Helipad comes with a free helicopter and a new Airfield with a free jet. With Auto Attack on, helicopters and jets do not wait for a group: each takes off as soon as it is built and flies back to rearm before rejoining the attack.
- **The computer plays differently by difficulty.** It does not wait for groups of 8: each wave is planned ahead, an ordinary wave or a big push, marching in one of several formations (line abreast, column, arrowhead, echelon or a loose skirmish line), sometimes split into a pincer that comes from two sides at once, by whichever crossing it picks. Between waves, small raids of its fastest units go after Ore Trucks and outlying sites. It keeps adding defences as it grows, facing the way your attacks have come from. On Normal and Hard it also answers your aircraft: once it sees them it builds more rocket soldiers, Mammoths or Rangers and fighters, puts AA sites beside the refineries, power plants and Outposts your aircraft strike, keeps a few anti-aircraft soldiers at home and holds fighters back on its Airfield to scramble against raids. *Easy*: waves of 4 to 7 in simple formations, mostly by the shortest route, at the Construction Yard; up to 4 ground defences. *Normal*: waves of 5 to 10 and big pushes of 10 to 13 (sometimes with Paratroopers), occasional pincers, any crossing (shorter ones more likely), raids from about 4 minutes, mixed targets (Construction Yard, refineries, power, Outposts); up to 8 ground defences, a gun at each Outpost and up to 3 AA sites once you fly aircraft. *Hard*: waves of 6 to 12 and big pushes of 12 to 16 timed with Paratroopers and Bombing Runs, frequent pincers by the least defended crossing, raids from about 3 minutes, aimed at your least defended valuable buildings; up to 12 ground defences, guns at Outposts and outlying refineries and up to 5 AA sites.
- **Base and Ore Truck defence.** Idle units waiting near a base attack any enemy ground units that get near or inside it, or that shoot at an Ore Truck nearby, with Auto Attack on or off. Each goes for the nearest raider it can hit, and they stay on it until the area has been quiet for a few seconds before returning to where they were (attack waves never pull defenders away mid-fight). Units you have sent somewhere stay on their orders. The computer also recalls attack groups still on their way out.
- **Aircraft on the ground and shot down.** Helicopters and jets each have a home Helipad or Airfield: new aircraft are based at whichever pad has the fewest, and move to the least crowded one left if theirs is lost. They park there, each in its own slot, with ammunition pips filling up as they rearm. Ground crews also repair damage while they are parked, quickly (a full repair takes about 12 seconds and costs half the aircraft's price); with no money they take off unrepaired. A badly damaged aircraft (under 35% health) on auto attack or guard flies home and stays on its pad until fully repaired; if money runs short, repairs pause until the Ore Trucks bring more in. Give it a direct order to send it out early. With Auto Attack off they wait there until ordered or until the base is attacked. When one is shot down its pilot bails out, parachutes down and runs for the nearest friendly structure without stopping to fight (reaching it recovers the pilot for $100; giving the pilot an order takes control of it instead), and the aircraft falls trailing smoke, crashes and leaves a burning wreck.
- **Buildings** clank and hammer as they go up. A destroyed structure goes down in a chain of explosions with a crash of collapsing metal and a screen shake, and a few soldiers (more from bigger buildings) stumble out of the wreck and join the defence.
- **Idle soldiers** fidget while they wait: they look around, rest or shoulder their weapons, check their sights, stretch, the grenadier tosses a grenade from hand to hand, and after a long quiet spell some drop for push-ups.
- **Infantry take cover** when they come under fire: a mix of staying upright, crouching (15% less damage) and going prone (35% less), until they move or the shooting stops.
- **A battlefield that remembers.** Infantry die according to what hit them, with recorded death cries: each body is a small physics ragdoll, so no two fall the same way. Shot soldiers are knocked back or slump forward, crumpling at the knees or going down stiff; blasts throw them tumbling through the air; flame can send a soldier staggering a few burning steps first, and a Tesla bolt makes him convulse before he drops, blackened. Weapons fly loose, and a small pool of blood spreads out from under each body once it lies still. The bodies stay where they fall (lying on top of any craters), and destroyed vehicles and downed aircraft leave blackened wrecks (about one tank in three cooks off: its ammunition goes up, flame roars out of the hull while rounds keep popping, and the turret is blown spinning into the air and crashes down beside the hull) that smoulder for a while and stay for the rest of the game, and shells, rockets, grenades and bombs leave craters. Both are painted into the ground, so a long battle costs no extra performance.
- **Jets**: the Allied Strike Jet is modelled on the F-15E Strike Eagle (twin engines, twin tails, two-seat canopy); the Soviet MiG is a single-engined delta. Both carry ground-attack missiles, air-to-air missiles for dogfights and bombs, shown as yellow, blue and grey pips while they rearm. They drop flares (two bursts per sortie) when a missile or rocket closes in: five bright flares released one after another as the jet flies on, each trailing smoke as it slows and sinks, which can pull the missile off course (flak still hits), and head home to rearm once their ground-attack missiles and bombs are spent, firing any air-to-air missiles left at enemy aircraft met on the way.
- **Airfield support powers.** Owning an Airfield charges three powers at the top of the left sidebar column: a **Spy Plane** (60 s) that reveals a wide area, **Paratroopers** (150 s) that drop a squad of five infantry who, once down, attack the nearest enemy structure and then the next nearest, fighting whatever comes at them, and a **Bombing Run** (200 s) that carpet-bombs a strip of ground. They charge at half speed on low power. Tap one when it shows READY, tap the target (tap again or press LAUNCH to confirm on touch screens), and a plane flies in from behind your base. AA guns and SAM sites can shoot the planes down. The computer uses Paratroopers and Bombing Runs too.
- Keep power above demand, or production slows and Tesla Coils go dark.
- Destroy the enemy Construction Yard to win; lose yours and the mission is over. Construction Yards cannot be sold.

## Field Manual

The main menu (and the pause menu) has a **Field Manual** button: a multi-page booklet in the style of the printed manuals of mid-90s strategy games. It covers the mission, controls and battle basics, then every structure, infantry unit, vehicle, aircraft and Airfield power with its portrait, side, cost, armour, speed, weapon, range, requirements and what it is strong and weak against, then the enemy commander by difficulty and field tips. The numbers are read from the game's own tables, so they always match.

## Sound

Battle sound effects are freely licensed recordings (CC0 and CC-BY, credited in [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md) and the in-game Credits), processed by `scripts/make_sfx.py`; interface sounds are synthesised live with the Web Audio API. Weapons, impacts and explosions are positioned by the camera (louder on screen, panned left or right). Fighting anywhere else on the map stays faintly audible as a muffled rumble from its direction, with its own share of sound channels so a big fight on screen never silences it. Announcements (a female EVA-style voice) and unit replies (men's voices: riflemen on a field radio, tank crews over a muffled intercom, pilots on an aircraft radio) are pre-recorded with the open-source [Kokoro](https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx) neural voice (Apache-2.0), processed with ffmpeg and embedded in `src/voices.js`. `scripts/make_voices.py` regenerates them. The soundtrack is four original pieces in a 90s industrial-metal style, written as a homage to classic RTS scores: *Night Sortie* (a slow, smooth half-time groove with strings and a brass melody), *Iron Fist March*, *Grind Protocol* and *Cold Front*, all slow and smooth. The sequencer plays them on recorded instruments (a real drum kit, double-tracked distorted guitars, picked bass, trumpet and horn leads, trombone stabs, string sections, anvil and timpani; all CC0 or CC-BY and credited), rendered into `src/music.js` by `scripts/make_music.py`. Music, effects and voice can be switched off in Options. On iPhone the game asks for playback audio, so it plays even with the silent switch on.

## Controls

| Action | Touch | Mouse / keyboard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Select | Tap a unit; double-tap for all of that type on screen | Click; double click |
| Select a group | Hold, then drag (or toggle ⬚ and drag) | Drag a box |
| Move / attack | Tap the ground / an enemy | Click the ground / an enemy |
| Deselect | ✕ button | Right click, Esc |
| Build | Tap a sidebar icon; when READY, tap the map to drop a ghost, tap it again to build | Click the icon, then click the map |
| Cancel production | Hold the icon | Right click the icon |
| Scroll / zoom | Drag / pinch, or tap the radar | Arrow keys or WASD, mouse wheel, click the radar |

## Play

- **Locally:** serve the folder (`python3 -m http.server`) and open `http://localhost:8000`.
- **On iPhone:** host the folder anywhere with HTTPS (GitHub Pages works: *Settings → Pages → Deploy from branch*), open it in Safari, then *Share → Add to Home Screen*. It launches full screen and works offline after the first visit. Landscape gives the biggest battlefield; portrait moves the sidebar to the bottom.

## Files

- `src/data.js`: unit, structure, faction and damage tables
- `src/voxel.js`: the voxel renderer (lighting, ambient occlusion, shadows, supersampling)
- `src/models.js`: voxel models for every unit, structure, tree, rock and ore pile
- `src/art.js`: terrain painting, sprite drawing, explosions, smoke and sidebar icons
- `src/world.js`: map generation, pathfinding, combat, harvesting, production, shroud
- `src/ai.js`: the computer opponent
- `src/audio.js`: sound effect, voice and instrument playback, and the music sequencer
- `src/sfx.js`: the recorded battle sound effects (generated by `scripts/make_sfx.py`)
- `src/music.js`: the recorded soundtrack instruments (generated by `scripts/make_music.py`)
- `src/voices.js`: the pre-recorded voice lines (generated by `scripts/make_voices.py`)
- `src/manual.js`: the Field Manual
- `src/ui.js`: rendering, touch and mouse input, sidebar and menus
- `sw.js`, `manifest.webmanifest`, `icons/`: offline support and home-screen install
- `scripts/build-single-file.py`: bundles everything into one HTML file
